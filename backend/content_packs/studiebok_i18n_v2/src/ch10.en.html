TITLE: Chapter 10 — Alcohol, drugs and driving

Driving under the influence is one of the most serious offences under the Road Traffic Act and one of the most frequent causes of serious accidents.

Road Traffic Act § 22. Influence of alcohol: No one may drive or attempt to drive a motor vehicle while under the influence of alcohol or any other intoxicating or anaesthetic substance. Limits:
  • Blood alcohol concentration above 0.2 per mille = always considered under the influence
  • Alternatively: alcohol concentration in exhaled breath above 0.1 mg per litre of air
The driver of a motor vehicle must not consume alcohol during the first six hours after finishing the drive, when he understands or must understand that a police investigation may follow.
Road Traffic Act § 22a. Breath test: The police may breath-test the driver of a motor vehicle when:
  • There is reason to believe he has violated § 22
  • He is involved in a traffic accident
  • He has been stopped in a traffic check

Penalties by blood alcohol level (§ 31):

  • 0.2 – 0.5 per mille: Fine
  • 0.5 – 1.0 per mille: Fine and suspended prison sentence
  • 1.0 – 1.5 per mille: Fine and suspended or unconditional prison sentence
  • Above 1.5 per mille: Fine and unconditional prison sentence
Road Traffic Act § 33. Loss of driving licence: The right to drive a motor vehicle is revoked for at least 1 year for drink-driving above 0.5 per mille. If repeated within 5 years: loss of the driving licence for life.
Tip: 0.2 per mille is very low — roughly one small beer for an average person. The safest approach is to NEVER drink alcohol if you are going to drive. Remember also that many common medicines can make driving illegal — always check with a doctor or pharmacy.
Road Traffic Act § 21: No one may drive a vehicle when he is ill, weakened, exhausted or tired in such a way that the driving cannot be done safely.
