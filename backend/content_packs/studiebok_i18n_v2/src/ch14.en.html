TITLE: Chapter 14 — Vehicles, technical condition and safety

A vehicle in good technical condition is fundamental to road safety.

Road Traffic Act § 13. Vehicle requirements: Vehicles must be built, designed, equipped and maintained so that they can be used without causing unnecessary danger or inconvenience and without damaging the road. Modifications that increase a motorcycle's maximum speed or performance beyond the set limits are forbidden.

Safety check of the car — what to inspect:

  • Brakes — test the brakes, check the braking effect
  • Wheels and tyres — tread depth (at least 1.6 mm, 3 mm recommended), tyre pressure, damage
  • Lights — all lights working (low beam, high beam, brake lights, indicators, rear lights)
  • Visibility — clean windows, mirrors correctly adjusted, windscreen wipers working
  • Steering — check for unusual sounds or stiffness
  • Warning equipment — warning triangle, reflective vest
  • Safety equipment — seat belts working and undamaged

Active and passive safety:

  • Active safety — equipment that prevents accidents: ABS brakes, electronic stability control (ESP), automatic speed adaptation, alcohol interlock, navigation system, adaptive cruise control
  • Passive safety — equipment that protects in an accident: seat belt (with pretensioner), airbags, head restraint (prevents whiplash), crumple zones in the body

Safety equipment and correct use:

  • Seat belt must always be used. The belt must sit over the shoulder and hip, not behind the back or under the arm. The driver is responsible for passengers under 15 wearing a belt.
  • Head restraint must be adjusted so that its top edge is level with the top of the head.
  • Child restraints — children under 15 and/or under 135 cm: an approved child seat is mandatory. The airbag MUST be deactivated when a child seat is in the front passenger seat.
Road Traffic Act § 13a: In a motor vehicle it is forbidden to possess or use equipment intended to warn of or interfere with traffic checks (radar detectors).
Tip: Do the safety check BEFORE the trip — not while driving. A quick check of lights, tyres and brakes can prevent accidents and expensive fines.

Environmentally friendly and economical driving:

  • Use the highest possible gear at moderate speed
  • Avoid unnecessary acceleration and hard braking — plan your driving
  • Correct tyre pressure reduces fuel consumption
  • Switch off the engine during long stops
  • Adapt your speed to the traffic — avoid stop-and-go
