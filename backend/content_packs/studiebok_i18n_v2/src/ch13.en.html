TITLE: Chapter 13 — Traffic accidents and first aid

Knowing what to do at a traffic accident can save lives. Here are the most important duties and procedures.

Your duties at a traffic accident (Road Traffic Act § 12):

  • Stop immediately — whether you are at fault or not
  • Help the injured — give help to injured people and animals
  • Give your identity — name and address to the others involved
  • Notify the police — as soon as possible in case of death or serious personal injury
  • Secure the scene — move vehicles that are a danger, but do not touch any traces

Procedure as the first person at the accident scene:

  1. Stop safely — park securely, switch on the hazard lights
  2. Set out the warning triangle — at least 50–150 m behind the accident
  3. Assess the situation — how many injured, what kind of injuries
  4. Call 113 (ambulance) — give the exact position and the number of injured
  5. Give life-saving first aid — consciousness, breathing, open airway

Basic first aid at a traffic accident:

  • Check consciousness: talk to the injured person, shake the shoulders gently
  • Open the airway: tilt the head gently back, lift the chin
  • Check breathing: look, listen and feel for a maximum of 10 seconds
  • Start CPR if breathing is not normal: 30 chest compressions + 2 rescue breaths
  • Unconscious but breathing normally: place in the recovery position
  • Do not move injured people unnecessarily (risk of neck/back injury)
1.5 First aid — Goal: The learner shall have knowledge of duties at traffic accidents, know the routines for securing and behaving at an accident scene, and be able to assess the extent of injuries and give practical first aid.
Tip: Remember the order: Alert — Secure — Help (Varsle — Sikre — Hjelpe). Always assess the danger to yourself and others before approaching the accident scene. Your safety matters!
