TITLE: Chapter 9 — Lights, signals and indications

Correct use of lights and signals makes you visible and lets you communicate with other road users.

Traffic Rules § 14. Signals and indications:
  • Unnecessary or inconsiderate use of sound or light signals is forbidden.
  • When turning or otherwise significantly changing the vehicle's lateral position, a signal must be given as guidance for other road users.
  • The driver of an emergency vehicle must give warning with a flashing blue light. A sound signal may be used in addition, but only when necessary.
  • Vehicles at road works that deviate from road traffic provisions must give warning with a flashing yellow light.
Traffic Rules § 15. Use of lights:
  • While driving a motor vehicle, the required high beam, low beam or approved daytime running lights must always be on.
  • High beam must not be used in a way that dazzles other road users.
  • Low beam must be used when high beam is not required or not permitted.
  • Cornering/fog lights cannot be used together with low beam.

Light types and when to use them:

  • Daytime running lights — always on while driving (lower intensity than low beam)
  • Low beam — standard lighting when driving, especially important in the dark and in poor visibility
  • High beam — gives longer range, but must not dazzle. Dip for oncoming traffic
  • Parking lights — used when parked in the dark when visibility is limited
Tip: Remember that signalling (indicators) is mandatory when changing lanes, turning and parking. Signal in good time — others need time to react!
Traffic Rules § 16. Disturbing driving: In or near built-up areas there must be no unnecessary and disturbing driving with a motor vehicle, use of the engine while idling, or use that causes unnecessary noise or emission of smoke or gas.
