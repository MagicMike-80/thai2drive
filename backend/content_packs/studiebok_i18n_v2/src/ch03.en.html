TITLE: Chapter 3 — Position on the road

Correct positioning on the road contributes to safe and efficient traffic flow. You find the rules in Sections 4 and 5 of the Traffic Rules.

§ 4. Use of the carriageway: Drivers must use the carriageway. It is forbidden to drive on the pavement or footpath. Drivers other than cyclists must not use cycle paths or cycle lanes. On motorways and motor traffic roads, only motor vehicles that can legally be driven at a minimum of 40 km/h are allowed. Mopeds must not be driven on motorways.
§ 5. The vehicle's position on the road:
  • As far as conditions allow, vehicles must be driven on the right-hand side of the road.
  • On a carriageway with two or more lanes in the direction of travel, the right lane must be used unless the traffic rules require or permit use of a lane to the left.
  • The vehicle must be kept well within the traffic lane.
  • Bicycles may be ridden on the road's right shoulder.
  • The distance to the vehicle in front must be large enough that there is no danger of a collision if the vehicle in front slows down or stops.
§ 6. Turning:
  • Turning right: drive as close to the right edge of the carriageway as possible
  • Turning left: drive as close to the middle of the carriageway as possible
  • On a carriageway with two or more lanes, move into the correct lane in good time
  • Vehicles coming from opposite directions may turn left in front of each other
§ 8. Changing lanes: A driver who wants to change lanes must give way to vehicles already in the lane they are moving into. When the number of lanes is reduced, speed must be mutually adjusted so that vehicles can continue alternately (the zipper principle).
Tip: Positioning is about creating safety zones around your car. Good positioning gives you and others more time to react.
