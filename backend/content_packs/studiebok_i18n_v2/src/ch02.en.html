TITLE: Chapter 2 — Definitions and road references

To understand the traffic rules, it is important to know the official definitions from Section 1 of the Traffic Rules.

§ 1a) Road: Public or private road, street or square (including storage areas, parking lots, bus stops, bridges, winter roads, ferry quays or other quays directly connected to a road) that is open to general traffic.
§ 1b) Road junction: A place where a road crosses or joins another road.
§ 1c) Carriageway: The part of the road intended for normal driving.
§ 1d) Traffic lane: Each of the longitudinal lanes into which a carriageway is divided by road markings, or which is wide enough for traffic with one line of cars.
§ 1e) Shoulder: The part of the road outside the edge line.
§ 1f) Footpath and cycle path: A road designated by official traffic signs for pedestrians, cyclists or combined pedestrian and cycle traffic. The path is separated from other roads by a lawn, ditch, fence, kerbstone or in some other way.
§ 1g) Cycle lane: A traffic lane designated for cyclists by official traffic signs and road markings.
§ 1h) Pavement (sidewalk): A facility for pedestrians separated from the carriageway by a kerbstone.
§ 1j) Level crossing: A crossing at the same level between a road and a railway or tramway on a separate track bed.
§ 1k) Parking: Any placing of a vehicle, even if the driver does not leave it. The exception is the shortest possible stop for passengers getting in or out, or for loading or unloading.
Tip: Note that parking also applies when you are sitting in the car. Only the shortest possible stop for getting in/out or loading is not counted as parking.

Section 2 of the Traffic Rules specifies who the rules apply to:

  • All traffic on roads
  • Riders and persons leading animals
  • A pedestrian also includes anyone who: skis, uses a wheelchair, walks a bicycle, pushes a pram or uses a toy vehicle
