TITLE: Chapter 15 — Risk, accidents and young drivers

Knowledge of risk and accident statistics helps you understand why the rules exist and make better decisions.

What is risk?

Risk is a measure that combines the probability and the consequence of an event. An event can carry high risk because it is very likely, or because the consequences would be catastrophic even if it is unlikely.

Accident statistics:

  • Young people are over-represented in traffic accidents
  • Apart from suicide, traffic accidents are the most common cause of death among boys aged 15 to 24
  • Nearly 80 per cent of those killed in traffic are men
  • The first 9 months with a driving licence: 40 times higher risk of running off the road
  • As driving becomes automatic, the risk drops sharply

Most common causes of accidents among young drivers:

  • Excessive speed — inability to judge the right speed
  • Fatigue and lack of sleep
  • Alcohol and drugs
  • Inattention (mobile phone, passengers)
  • Poor weather and road grip
  • Overtaking in the wrong place

The driving process — SEE, UNDERSTAND, CHOOSE:

  • SEE — systematically and actively gather information about the traffic environment. Use the mirrors, look far ahead, keep your eyes moving.
  • UNDERSTAND — interpret what you see. What does it mean? What can happen? What danger is present?
  • CHOOSE — choose the right action: speed, position, signal/indication.
Tip: A good driver is defensive and anticipating — not necessarily the fastest. Consideration and helpfulness contribute more to road safety than driving technique alone.

Factors that negatively affect your driving ability:

  • Fatigue — after 17 hours without sleep you are as impaired as at 0.5 per mille
  • Mobile phone/distraction — the risk of an accident increases 4–8 times
  • Alcohol and drugs
  • Strong emotions (anger, grief, stress)
  • Illness and medication

Vulnerable road users — who is extra exposed in traffic:

  • Children — unpredictable, low visibility, lack of traffic understanding
  • The elderly — reduced vision, hearing and reaction ability
  • Cyclists and pedestrians — little protection in a collision
  • Motorcyclists — highly exposed in a collision
